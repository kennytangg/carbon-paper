\begin{abstract}
Satellite platforms promise to replace field inventories in forest carbon crediting, but their estimates are rarely tested blind against the standard-formula answer, and credits are issued with deductions of unknown risk. A right grid computes sealed field ground truth to SNI 7724, IPCC, CDM, and Verra rules; an isolated left grid sees only plot coordinates and dates and estimates carbon from time-matched Sentinel-1/2, Landsat, PALSAR, canopy height, and GEDI lidar. On 476 plots, blind error was 21\% on 1\,ha plots and 7--9\% on project totals outside Indonesia, but near 100\% on small Indonesian plots. Counting trees on a 1\,m canopy height map found 3\% of the stems and 25\% of the carbon (62\% on the 2026 map). In 1{,}000 simulated projects the best map found a median 95\% of the field carbon; satellite embeddings and a regional bias correction raised the share within $\pm25$\% from 80\% to 88.5\%, and on 35 unseen Bornean plots the frozen method found 85\%. Only 21.5\% of Sentinel-2 acquisitions see an Indonesian plot clear. We propose conformal crediting, a lower bound calibrated on other projects with field truth. It met a 10\% over-crediting target for five maps within 2.9 points, was invariant to map inflation, and credited 72--84\% of the true stock without a field plot, while the VM0047 deduction over-credited 23--29\% of projects that had field plots. No satellite estimate of annual growth beat assuming no change.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A forest carbon credit is issued on a number computed by a fixed chain: field crews measure each stem's diameter $D$ and height $H$ and identify its species for wood density $\rho$, a pantropical allometric equation converts these to aboveground biomass (AGB)~\cite{chave2014improved}, and fixed factors convert AGB to carbon and CO$_2$~\cite{verra2025vm0047,ipcc2019v4}. The costly part is the field visit, above all in remote Indonesian forests. Satellite platforms, including our NusaCarbon prototype, promise to replace it, and global biomass maps exist~\cite{santoro2021global,chloris2022}, but unverifiable estimates produce over-crediting~\cite{west2023redd}.

How close is a satellite number to the standard formula's at the same place and time? Three traps inflate reported accuracy: \emph{leakage}, training or tuning on plots next to those scored~\cite{ploton2020spatial}; \emph{temporal mismatch} of field and image dates; and \emph{scale}, comparing 0.05\,ha plots with 10\,m to 4.6\,km pixels. An audit of our own platform found forest figures driven by default constants, scene-level cloud screening, and an unchecked metadata ``confidence'' grade.

We separate the problem into two grids (Fig.~\ref{fig:grids}). The \textbf{right grid} is fixed: public field plots, the standard formula, a carbon density. The \textbf{left grid} is free, any satellite data and algorithm, but never sees the right grid's outcomes for the plots it is scored on. We ask: \textbf{RQ1} (methodology) how far a blind satellite estimate deviates from the ground truth, by plot size and aggregation; \textbf{RQ2} (AI) which inputs and learners close the gap, and whether trees can be counted from space; \textbf{RQ3} how often clouds hide an Indonesian plot, and whether uncertainty can be stated honestly; \textbf{RQ4} whether a credit can be issued at a stated risk of over-crediting, and growth estimated; and \textbf{RQ5} (distributed systems) whether extraction can be sharded and fault tolerant with bit-identical output.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) A blind, time-matched protocol with a commit-reveal ledger (476 stock, 196 repeat-census plots). (ii) A standards-based right grid with a Monte Carlo uncertainty budget. (iii) A plot-level clear-sky climatology for Indonesia. (iv) A test of missing-data conformal intervals under cloud. (v) \emph{Conformal crediting}, a crediting rule with calibrated over-crediting risk, invariant to map inflation, built on standard conformal machinery~\cite{lei2018distribution}. (vi) A tree-by-tree satellite estimator and 1{,}000 simulated projects. (vii) Fixes for each limit, frozen and tested blind on 35 unseen plots. (viii) A sharded, idempotent extraction runner.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Remote sensing.} Optical and radar signals saturate over dense canopy. GEDI lidar samples canopy height in 25\,m footprints~\cite{duncanson2022gedi}; global maps include ESA CCI Biomass~\cite{santoro2021global}, Chloris~\cite{chloris2022}, and 1\,m and 10\,m canopy height~\cite{tolan2024meta,lang2023eth}. \textbf{Validation and uncertainty.} BIOMASS propagates the uncertainty of Chave's allometry~\cite{rejou2017biomass}. Conformal prediction gives finite-sample intervals~\cite{lei2018distribution}, conformalized quantile regression (CQR) adapts their width~\cite{romano2019cqr}, and Zaffran \textit{et al.} handle missing covariates~\cite{zaffran2023conformal}. Conservative crediting asks that a credit exceed the truth with small probability~\cite{grassi2008applying}, solved parametrically by Neeff~\cite{neeff2021risk}.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Right Grid: Ground Truth by the Standards}
\label{sec:right}
\textbf{Data.} The stock benchmark has 476 plots in 27 region blocks: 148 HCSA plots in six Indonesian provinces, nested 0.01/0.05\,ha~\cite{hcsa2023data}; 142 GEO-TREES network plots, pantropical~\cite{geotrees2024}; and 186 Labri\`ere 1\,ha units in French Guiana and Gabon~\cite{labriere2018insitu}. For change, the SAFE census in Sabah gives 25$\times$25\,m plots measured stem by stem with day-precision dates from 2010 to 2020~\cite{svatek2025safe}. We follow SNI 7724~\cite{sni7724} (five pools, precision $\le$20\%), the HCSA toolkit~\cite{rosoman2017hcsa}, IPCC~\cite{ipcc2019v4} (Monte Carlo allowed), CDM AR-TOOL14~\cite{artool14}, Verra VM0047~\cite{verra2025vm0047} (deduct $U-10\%$), and Gold Standard~\cite{gs2024ar} (deduct the excess over 20\%).

\textbf{What a plot is.} Coordinates only locate a plot; the ground truth is the list of trees inside it. A crew tags every stem above the minimum diameter, measures its diameter $D$ at 1.3\,m and its height $H$ with a clinometer, and records its local name, which a botanist resolves to species. Wood density $\rho$, oven-dry mass per green volume, is looked up by species, genus, or family. HCSA publishes the list: 2{,}528 stems of 370 species in 89 families, each with $D$, $H$, name, and ICRAF $\rho$~\cite{hcsa2023data}; GEO-TREES and Labri\`ere publish plot biomass. None of it reaches the left grid.

\textbf{Tree, plot, and carbon.} A tree's AGB follows Chave~2014 Eq.~4~\cite{chave2014improved},
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AGB}_i=0.0673\,(\rho_i D_i^2 H_i)^{0.976}\ \text{[kg]},\qquad \sigma_{\ln}=0.357,
\label{eq:chave4}
\end{equation}
with $H$ measured (HCSA) or from a local $\ln H$--$\ln D$ model (Labri\`ere)~\cite{rejou2017biomass}. Tree biomass is expanded by the area in which a stem of its size is counted, and pools and carbon follow IPCC and SNI 7724:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AGB}_j=\sum_{i\in j}\frac{\mathrm{AGB}_i}{1000\,a(D_i)},\qquad C_j=\mathrm{CF}\,(1+R)\,\mathrm{AGB}_j,
\label{eq:plot}
\end{equation}
with $a(D)=0.01$\,ha for $5\le D<15$\,cm and 0.05\,ha above, CO$_2$e$_j=\frac{44}{12}C_j$, CF $=0.47$, and root-to-shoot $R=0.37$ (SNI 7724, IPCC 2006) or 0.21 (IPCC 2019). We compare AGB, so CF and $R$ cancel and every relative error holds for carbon and CO$_2$e. HCSA was recomputed tree by tree; plots with a missing input or an implausible stem (a \textit{Gluta renghas} at $D=480$\,cm) were excluded, never imputed, as were palms, for which Eq.~\ref{eq:chave4} is not made.

\textbf{Uncertainty and deduction.} Plots are grouped into strata $h$ with weights $w_h$, and the project uncertainty at 90\% is $u=t_{0.95}\sqrt{\sum_h w_h^2s_h^2/n_h}/\bar b$ (AR-TOOL14)~\cite{artool14}. AR-TOOL14 counts sampling error only. Measurement and model errors are large (the allometric residual alone is $\sigma_{\ln}=0.357$ per tree) and pass through the nonlinear Eq.~\ref{eq:chave4}, where summing variances (IPCC Approach~1) is inexact, so we use Approach~2, Monte Carlo: 2{,}000 draws per tree of diameter, height, wood density, allometric residual, and CF errors with the BIOMASS error models~\cite{rejou2017biomass}. For change, VM0047 Eq.~28 gives $U=T\sqrt{SE_0^2+SE_1^2-2\rho\,SE_0SE_1}/\Delta C$, $\rho$ the correlation of the two stock means, and credits are multiplied by $1-\min(1,\max(0,U-0.1))$~\cite{verra2025vm0047}.

\subsection{Left Grid: Inputs and Estimators}
From coordinates and the field date $t_j$ only, the left grid reads clear observations within $t_j\pm90$\,days: Sentinel-2 L2A bands and indices, Landsat 5--9, Sentinel-1 backscatter, the annual ALOS PALSAR mosaic, and elevation; through Google Earth Engine also the Meta 1\,m and ETH 10\,m canopy height maps~\cite{tolan2024meta,lang2023eth}, GEDI L4A biomass and L2A height within 1\,km~\cite{duncanson2022gedi}, ESA CCI Biomass~\cite{santoro2021global}, and Hansen loss~\cite{hansen2013high}; the fixes of Section~\ref{sec:prod} add the AlphaEarth embedding (64 features per 10\,m pixel)~\cite{brown2025alphaearth} and the 2026 canopy height map~\cite{brandt2026chmv2}. \textbf{E0} reads published products as they are. \textbf{E2} is LightGBM~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} on $\ln\mathrm{AGB}$ with smearing retransformation, trained on all region blocks but the target (leave-one-region-out, LORO). \textbf{E3} recalibrates E2 by the ratio on $k$ random plots of the target block. We score relative RMSE (rRMSE), bias, $R^2$, and the error of the mean of $n$ plots, a project total, with a region-block bootstrap (2{,}000 draws) over five seeds.

\textbf{From pixels to carbon.} A satellite does not see trees; it records reflected light and radar echoes per pixel (Fig.~\ref{fig:pixels}). Optical bands become indices such as the normalized difference vegetation index~\cite{tucker1979red}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NDVI}=(R_{\mathrm{NIR}}-R_{\mathrm{red}})/(R_{\mathrm{NIR}}+R_{\mathrm{red}}),
\label{eq:ndvi}
\end{equation}
with $R$ the reflectance in Sentinel-2 bands B8 (842\,nm) and B4 (665\,nm): chlorophyll absorbs red and leaves reflect near-infrared, so a green canopy reads about 0.8 and bare soil 0.1--0.2. The estimator (1)~reads every clear pixel of the plot window within $\pm90$\,days, (2)~summarises each input into features (band medians, seven indices, backscatter, canopy height percentiles, GEDI biomass), (3)~predicts $\ln\mathrm{AGB}$ with a model trained on other regions, and (4)~converts with $C=0.47\,\mathrm{AGB}$ and CO$_2$e$\,=\frac{44}{12}C$.

\textbf{E4: count the trees, then the formula.} E4 rebuilds the right grid's tree list from the 1\,m canopy height map, smoothed by a 1\,m Gaussian. Tree tops are local maxima above 5\,m in a window of radius $0.06H+0.5$\,m~\cite{popescu2004seeing}; a crown grows from each top while the canopy stays above half its height; and the stem diameter follows from height and crown diameter $CD$ by the tropical-angiosperm model of Jucker \textit{et al.}~\cite{jucker2017allometric},
\begin{equation}
D=a\,(H\cdot CD)^{b}\ \text{[cm]},\qquad (a,b)=(0.486,\,0.802),
\label{eq:jucker}
\end{equation}
for Indo-Malaya (0.428, 0.821 Afrotropic; 0.591, 0.781 Neotropic). A satellite cannot identify species, so every tree takes its region's mean tropical $\rho$ in the Global Wood Density Database (0.574 for Southeast Asia)~\cite{rejou2017biomass}, and Eq.~\ref{eq:chave4} gives its biomass. E4 uses no field data. \emph{E4 scaled} makes up for the trees E4 cannot see: it multiplies E4 by the ratio of field to E4 carbon on the other regions.

\subsection{Clouds}
For 97 locations we read the Sentinel-2 scene classification of all 22{,}697 acquisitions of 2021--2023 in a 5$\times$5 window of 20\,m pixels (\emph{clear} if every pixel is) and test the scene filter \texttt{CLOUDY\_PIXEL\_PERCENTAGE}~$<30$ against it. For a share $q$ of target plots we then remove the optical block and compare a naive model, modality dropout~\cite{neverova2016moddrop}, a radar model, and radar-imputed optical features.

\subsection{Confidence: Plot and Project Intervals}
All intervals target 90\% and are calibrated on inner LORO residuals of the training regions. CQR~\cite{romano2019cqr} scores $s=\max\{\hat q_{\mathrm{lo}}(x)-y,\,y-\hat q_{\mathrm{hi}}(x)\}$ on $y=\ln\mathrm{AGB}$ and widens the quantile band by the conformal quantile of $s$. \emph{Cloud-aware} CQR scores each calibration plot as observed and with its optical block removed, and gives each test plot the threshold of its missingness pattern (CP-MDA~\cite{zaffran2023conformal}); clouds are not independent of the outcome, so coverage must be measured. A project's plot errors share one regional bias, so $SE^2=s_w^2/n+s_b^2$, with $s_w$, $s_b$ the within- and between-region spread of log residuals elsewhere. With $k$ field plots, the satellite becomes auxiliary data for a ratio estimator.

\subsection{Conformal Crediting}
A registry issues a number, not an interval: a lower bound that should exceed the true stock in at most a share $\delta$ of projects~\cite{grassi2008applying,neeff2021risk}. A satellite map has no design-based standard error, so we calibrate the bound on other projects with field truth. Project $j$ has estimate $\hat Y_j$, true mean $Y_j$, relative standard error $r_j$ ($r_j=1$ if none), and score $z_j=\ln(\hat Y_j/Y_j)/r_j$. With $m$ calibration scores, a new project is credited
\begin{equation}
C=\hat Y\exp(-q\,r),\qquad q=z_{(\lceil (m+1)(1-\delta)\rceil)}.
\label{eq:cc}
\end{equation}
If projects are exchangeable, $P(C>Y)\le\delta$, as in split conformal prediction with projects as units~\cite{lei2018distribution}, however plots inside a project are correlated; it needs $m\ge1/\delta-1$, nine projects for $\delta=0.1$. Multiplying the map by $c$ shifts every plain score and $q$ by $\ln c$ and leaves $C$ unchanged: \emph{inflating a map earns no credit}. Regions of 16 or more plots are split into spatial sub-projects of about 10 plots (31 projects in 19 regions), each calibrated only on other regions. We score the over-crediting rate and the \emph{yield}, the mean share $C_j/Y_j$ credited.

\subsection{Ledger and Distributed Extraction}
Every protocol step is an entry in an append-only log carrying the SHA-256 of the previous entry~\cite{haber1991timestamp}: \texttt{seal}, \texttt{commit} (prediction file digests), \texttt{release}, and \texttt{reveal}, accepted only if the sealed file still matches; later commits are refused. An extraction task is one (plot, date, window, sensor) request stored under its hash, so re-execution is idempotent and the cache is a checkpoint. Transient errors are retried with capped backoff, and dense tiles are split and placed by consistent hashing~\cite{karger1997consistent} or the longest-processing-time (LPT) rule~\cite{graham1969bounds}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{How Certain Is the Right Grid?}
On the 148 HCSA plots, the Monte Carlo 95\% interval of plot AGB has a median half-width, half its length over the plot value, of 31\%: the field answer itself is known only to about $\pm31$\%, so ``100\% accuracy'' has no meaning. The standards differ too: SNI 7724's root-to-shoot ratio gives 13\% more carbon than the 2019 Refinement, and the same Jambi data are deducted 24\% (VM0047), 34\% (AR-TOOL14), or 14\% (Gold Standard). AR-TOOL14 counts sampling error only (9--34\% by province), though measurement and allometric error carry 10--64\% of the variance of the mean. For growth, VM0047 Eq.~28 with $\rho=0$ deducts all credit for 13 of 15 SAFE blocks; with the measured $\rho$ (0.45--0.99), seven keep some.

\subsection{Blind Accuracy (RQ1) and Closing the Gap (RQ2)}
Table~\ref{tab:acc} and Fig.~\ref{fig:sat}a give the blind errors. The confirmatory E2, committed before the reveal, missed by 48.5\% overall, 28.6\% on the 1\,ha units, 41.9\% on GEO-TREES, and 102.9\% on HCSA, where neither committed estimator beat predicting the mean. The largest gain came from inputs, not algorithms: adding the 1\,m canopy height map and GEDI cut the overall error by 9.0 points (bootstrap 95\% interval $-13.0$ to $-6.1$), to 20.6\% on the 1\,ha units and 27.7\% on GEO-TREES, and raised $R^2$ from 0.29 to 0.53. Read as published, GEDI under-estimated by 29--41\%: lidar helps as an input, not an answer.

\textbf{Where the error comes from.} \emph{Saturation:} predictions flatten near 300\Mgha{} (E2 mean 293 above 400, 60 below 50) because the inputs stop changing: from field means of 262 to 480\Mgha{} the median canopy height rose only from 19.6 to 21.7\,m and GEDI from 199 to 207\Mgha. Above 300\Mgha{} no input keeps a rank correlation with the field above 0.26, against up to 0.62 at 100--300, and radar is flat from 100 (Fig.~\ref{fig:sat}b), as reported before~\cite{rejou2019upscaling}; no model can separate what its inputs do not. \emph{Plot size:} the largest of a median 15 stems holds 24\% of an HCSA plot's AGB, and one 1\,m stem adds about 110\Mgha{} to a 0.05\,ha plot~\cite{chave2004error}.

\textbf{Aggregation and local plots.} With canopy height and GEDI and no field plot, the median error of a block total was 7\% on the 1\,ha units, 9\% on GEO-TREES, and 20\% on HCSA; a ratio on $k=10$--20 local plots (E3) took E2 totals from 29 to 9--10\% on GEO-TREES and 11 to 6\% on Labri\`ere, but hurt on HCSA until $k=20$.

\subsection{Counting Trees from Space, and Ten Projects}
\textbf{What the field counts.} The median HCSA plot holds 700 stems per hectare of five species, most of them small: stems of 5--15\,cm are 75\% of the count but 13\% of the carbon, and the 6\% of 30\,cm or more hold 58\%. Light wood ($\rho<0.5$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$, mostly Lauraceae and Moraceae) is 24\% of the stems and 16\% of the carbon, medium wood (0.5--0.7, led by Dipterocarpaceae) 44\% and 44\%, heavy wood ($\ge0.7$) 32\% and 40\%. The regional default $\rho$ instead of each species' value leaves 91\% of the total carbon, 78--115\% of a plot's (10th--90th percentile).

\textbf{What the satellite counts.} On the HCSA plots E4 found a median of 20 trees per hectare (Fig.~\ref{fig:pixels}c): 3\% of the stems, 11\% of those of 15\,cm or more. The map stores whole metres and is smoother than a crown; unsmoothed, every stair step becomes a top (270 per hectare), so the count is a processing choice. E4 found 25\% of the field carbon, 23--26\% in every dataset; scaled ($\times$3.8) it found 97\% of the total, but only 36\% of plots within $\pm25$\%, against 60\% for the map. NDVI (Eq.~\ref{eq:ndvi}) cannot do better: its median on 136 clear plots was 0.77 below 100\Mgha{} and 0.83 above 300.

\textbf{Ten projects.} Fig.~\ref{fig:sim} gives the carbon each grid finds in simulated 1{,}000\,ha projects of 10 random plots of one region. Over all 1{,}000 the map found a median 95\% of the field carbon, within $\pm25$\% in 80\% of projects; conformal crediting issued a median 86\% and exceeded the field in 12\%, near its 10\% target.

\subsection{Toward Production: Fixes and an Unseen Test}
\label{sec:prod}
We attacked each limit after the reveal (exploratory, Fig.~\ref{fig:prod}), then froze the method and tested it blind. \textbf{Inputs.} The embedding cut plot error from 40.1 to 35.8\% (Table~\ref{tab:acc}), from Earth Engine alone; the 2026 map added little (35.1\%). \textbf{Bias.} The map's bias repeats within a realm, so we multiply it by $e^{-\mu}$, $\mu$ the mean log map-to-field ratio of the realm's other regions: HCSA projects within $\pm25$\% rose from 56 to 68\%. Ten local plots added little (1\,ha) or noise (0.05\,ha). \textbf{Credit.} Errors are shared within a region, so one score per region in Eq.~\ref{eq:cc} cut over-crediting from 13.6 to 9.7\%. \textbf{Height.} On the 2026 map, 4\,m taller, E4 found 62\% of the carbon. Our platform's formula $3.836H^{1.131}$ is in no publication, applies 0.47 twice, and found 35\%. \textbf{Frozen method.} Per 1\,ha cell: Earth Engine features, the LightGBM map, the estimate $\hat Y e^{-\mu}$, the credit $\hat Y e^{-q}$ ($q$ over verified regions), and growth from the IPCC default (0.7\,t\,d.m.\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ primary, 2.7--3.4 secondary~\cite{ipcc2019v4}). \textbf{Unseen test.} 35 plots of 0.28\,ha measured in 2020 in Danum, Sabah~\cite{keller2023danum} (4{,}201 stems, 366\Mgha), were sealed before the frozen method, trained without the three GEO-TREES Danum plots, committed. The map found 64\% of the field carbon, the estimate 85\% (93\% of 10-plot draws within $\pm25$\%), and the credit 59\%, never above the field.

\subsection{Clouds and Confidence (RQ3)}
\textbf{How often is a plot clear?} At a median Indonesian location only 21.5\% of Sentinel-2 acquisitions see the plot clear (Americas 39\%), but revisit compensates: the median gap between clear looks is 10 days. \emph{The scene filter is the real problem}: in Indonesia it keeps 11.5\% of acquisitions, 34\% of them cloudy over the plot, and discards 65\% of the clear ones. Removing every optical feature raised the error only from 48.0 to 50.0\%, as the model leans on radar, terrain, and annual products; imputing optical features from radar was harmful (bias $-61$\%).

\textbf{Which intervals are honest?} Only CQR reaches the 90\% target with usable width (89.9\%, upper bound 5.3 times the lower); normalized conformal under-covers (79.4\%), as a difficulty model fitted elsewhere does not transfer. With every target plot clouded, plain CQR falls to 86.5\% and cloud-aware CQR keeps 90.5\% ($+4.0$ points, interval $+1.9$ to $+6.6$); HCSA stays under-covered (68--81\%). A metadata confidence score like our platform's carries no information about error once the dataset is controlled for (partial rank correlation $-0.03$ to $+0.07$).

\textbf{Projects.} For 19 projects, intervals assuming independent plot errors covered 74\% (HCSA 33\%), the two-component interval all 19. As auxiliary data the satellite cut the plots needed for $\pm$10\% from 408 to 216 (GEO-TREES) and 381 to 162 (Labri\`ere), none on HCSA.

\subsection{From Intervals to Credits and Growth (RQ4)}
We credit the 31 projects at $\delta=0.10$. Crediting the raw map over-credits 35.5\% of projects; VM0047 on the honest two-component uncertainty never over-credits but credits only 48\% of the true stock. Conformal crediting meets its target: 9.7\% of projects are over-credited, and it credits 72\% of the true stock without a field plot, 84\% with the canopy height and GEDI map. A better map buys credits, not risk: on five maps, including E2 inflated by 10 and 20\%, over-crediting stayed within 2.9 points of the target, and the inflated maps earned the same 72\%. With 5 random field plots and a ratio estimator, VM0047 still over-credits 22.6\% of projects (yield 82\%), 29\% with 10, as its deduction starts only above 10\% uncertainty; the design lower bound and studentized conformal meet the target (9.9 and 9.6\%, yield 81\%).

\textbf{Growth.} Excluding oil palm, 196 SAFE plots gained a mean 1.87\Mgha\,yr$^{-1}$. The Chloris difference had the error of predicting no change (RMSE 8.06 vs 8.12) and the E2 difference twice that: a 30--100\% stock error cannot resolve 1\% growth a year. The mean rate of the other blocks was best (block MAE 0.98 vs 2.05 for no change); PALSAR, CCI change, and Hansen loss did not beat it (1.20).

\subsection{Systems (RQ5)}
The stock extraction ran 2{,}052 tasks on 24 workers in 2.1\,h; splitting dense tiles cut the worst-worker load factor from 6.75 to 1.40 (LPT), and retries absorbed 85 transient errors. Results were bit-identical to the single-worker run at any worker count, under injected faults, and after a resume; both ledgers verify end to end.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Can satellites replace the field plot?} Not at plot scale, and not for growth. Open data put a 1\,ha plot within about 21\% (airborne lidar: 13\% in Sabah~\cite{jucker2018estimating}; radar: 31--38\% in Kalimantan~\cite{berninger2018sar}), a 0.05\,ha plot within about 100\%. Nor can they count trees: the 1\,m map shows a canopy surface, not stems, and the formula applied to it finds a quarter of the carbon, 62\% on the 2026 map. Satellites \emph{replace part of the inventory}: project totals, and half the field plots where plots are large.

\textbf{Confidence must be calibrated, not graded.} A metadata score said nothing about error, and the $U-10\%$ deduction is no lower confidence bound; conformal crediting makes the over-crediting rate a design parameter and removes the reward for inflating a map. Its novelty is narrow: the target is Grassi's and Neeff's, the machinery split conformal, and area-level bounds exist~\cite{zhang2026conformal}, but we found no crediting rule calibrated across projects on field truth.

\textbf{Implications and roadmap.} For NusaCarbon: the frozen method of Section~\ref{sec:prod} instead of the height formula, plot-level cloud masking, and cloud-aware CQR instead of a letter grade. Stems stay unseen, which motivates drone lidar and under-canopy scans of Indonesian 1\,ha plots.

\textbf{Threats to validity.} Only E0, E2, the first change estimates, and the Danum test are confirmatory; all other experiments, E4 included, are exploratory, and we report every variant run. Danum is one valley of tall forest. GEDI and Chloris may have seen some of our sites; HCSA and Labri\`ere dates are known to the year. Projects are region blocks, not registered projects.

%=====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
Tested blind against a sealed standard-formula right grid, open satellite data reached 21\% error on 1\,ha plots, 7--9\% on project totals, and a median 95\% of the field carbon in 1{,}000 simulated projects, but failed on small plots, tree counts, and growth, and their inputs saturate above 300\Mgha. Embeddings and a realm bias correction put 88.5\% of projects within $\pm25$\%, and the frozen method found 85\% on unseen Bornean plots. Plot-level cloud masking nearly triples usable optical data, cloud-aware CQR keeps intervals honest, and conformal crediting issues credits at a stated risk without a field plot.
